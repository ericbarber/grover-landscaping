# Imagery Direction

Grover imagery should feel editorial, regional, and observant. Landscapes are
shown as cared-for places and operating environments—not as luxury backdrops or
generic smiling-worker stock photography.

## Photography principles

- Natural Southwestern light and believable desert-adapted planting.
- Real materials: stone, soil, bark, worn tools, work fabric, and plant texture.
- Wide compositions with usable negative space when supporting marketing copy.
- People appear candidly doing real work; avoid staged gestures and close-up stock
  expressions.
- No visible third-party marks, license plates, uniforms, or vehicle branding.
- Avoid exaggerated HDR, artificial neon lawns, mansion emphasis, and fantasy
  planting combinations.

## Product imagery

- Show real interface states and plausible data instead of floating generic cards.
- Use before/after evidence only when both images show the same view and honest
  conditions.
- Customer proof should feel calm and trustworthy; manager recovery imagery should
  remain secondary to actionable state.
